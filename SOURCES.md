# Content provenance and review

Research date: 17 October 2026. All eight cases are original synthetic training cases, not copies of sample patients or claims about future questions. Patient names, histories and findings are fictional. Content needs independent clinical/examiner review before use as a formal teaching or assessment product.

## Official examination framework

- https://www.thefederation.uk/examinations/paces/format — authoritative current station order, encounter durations, 125-minute carousel, 6-minute clinical examination / 4-minute questioning, 15-minute consultation / 5-minute questioning, and no examiner questioning during communication encounters.
- https://www.thefederation.uk/examinations/paces/preparation — systematic examination, presentation and candidate preparation.
- https://www.thefederation.uk/examinations/paces/sample-scenarios — public scenario structures.
- https://www.thefederation.uk/sites/default/files/uploads/PACES23%20scenario%20videos%20-%20communication.pdf — reviewed the publicly released communication example to understand candidate/role-player/examiner separation, concern handling and closure. Patient facts and dialogue were not reproduced.
- https://www.mrcpuk.org/sites/default/files/documents/PACES23%20scenario%20videos%20-%20consultation.pdf — reviewed the public integrated consultation example, focused assessment and examiner discussion structure. No patient facts copied.

## Community review

https://www.reddit.com/r/MRCP/comments/1qe98c2/paces_experience/ — a publicly accessible personal account describing a December 2025 sitting. Used only to identify rehearsal themes: stress, communicating concerns and presenting positives and negatives. This account's examination location and author credentials are not verified.

https://www.reddit.com/r/doctorsUK/comments/1x70us4/neurology_for_paces/ — October 2026 search result about localisation, differential diagnosis and bedside practice. Full-page retrieval failed; treated as limited contextual evidence, not a case source.

No reliable collection of recent UK recalled cases was established. Accordingly, the app uses explicitly synthetic cases and makes no frequency or prediction claims. Community statements are not used as clinical guidance.
